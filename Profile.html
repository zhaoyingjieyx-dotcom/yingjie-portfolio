About Me

I am a first-year Ph.D. student at Virginia Tech, having previously earned my Master of Science degree at Beijing University of Civil Engineering and Architecture.

My research focuses on Structural Health Monitoring (SHM), anomaly detection, and machine learning applications. I have published 11 peer-reviewed journal articles, accumulating over 189 citations and an h-index of 6. My work is driven by the goal of developing low-cost, accurate computational frameworks to solve complex civil engineering problems.

Objective

As a first-year Ph.D. student, my immediate objective is to deepen my expertise in structural health monitoring and neural networks. Upon completing my doctoral degree, I intend to pursue a career in academia or a research-intensive industry role, focusing on designing robust, scalable, and intelligent structural monitoring systems. I aim to bridge the gap between advanced machine learning algorithms and practical civil/structural engineering applications.

Research Overview

My research centres on Structural Health Monitoring (SHM), abnormal data detection and cleaning, and deep learning for civil engineering. My work spans historic masonry structures, long-span bridges, and cable modal identification, with a consistent focus on developing low-cost, accurate frameworks that bridge advanced machine learning and practical structural monitoring.
